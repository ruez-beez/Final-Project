What is the Solar System?

A solar system is simply a way to refer to a system of planets. It’s called the “solar system” because ours revolves around the sun. Solar is a word to describe things that are related to our sun.

Where is the Solar System?

The solar system that surrounds us here on earth is located in the Milky Way galaxy. Its shape resembles a spiral that wraps around the sun. It contains eight planets, five dwarf planets, moons, asteroids, and comets.

Why does it matter to me?

Earth, the planet we live on, is located in this solar system. We are the third closest planet to the sun. The distance from the sun is related to the order of the planet's formation.

Mercury

Named after the fastest of the roman gods, Mercury is the smallest and closest to the sun. It’s one of four under the terrestrial planets group. This means that its surface is primarily solid and rocky. The terrain of Mercury is full of impact craters and basins. Its temperature varies wildly due to the lack of atmosphere that could balance out and retain heat. So it swings from an extreme of 800 F to -290 F. Rather than having an atmosphere, there is an exosphere. This exosphere is rather thin and made up of atoms from the debris of meteoroid impacts.

Venus

Venus, named after the Roman goddess of Love, is the hottest planet in our solar system. It wraps itself in a thick atmosphere that traps gases and heat. Its surface temperatures reach 872 F and the surface of Venus is littered with volcanoes and mountains. Venus’s skies are comprised of clouds and strange dark streaks, currently unexplainable by scientists. The orbit of venus is slow and lethargic, 1 venus day = 243 earth days. Although Venus has no moons, it does have a satellite named Zoozve.

Earth

Earth is the biggest of the terrestrial planets. It is also not named after a Roman or Greek god, rather, it has a name that is Germanic in origin. The structure of the earth is made up of layers; an inner core, mantle, and crust. The crust is where life on and our oceans earth resides on. The surface is varied and contains volcanoes, mountains, and valleys. Its surface is ever shifting atop of plates. Water covers the majority of the planet. The atmosphere is made up of mostly nitrogen and oxygen that helps protect the planet from the sun’s radiation.

Mars

Named after the Roman God of war, Mars is a red dusty planet. This red color comes from the oxidized iron minerals on its surface. The planet’s surface contains volcanos, canyons, and craters. The temperature is colder, reaching around 70 F to -225 F. It often has dust storms and makes the sky have a reddish hue. The atmosphere on mars is thin and made up of carbon dioxide, nitrogen, and argon gases. Mars also has two moons, called Phobos and Deimos. They are quite small and lumpy.
